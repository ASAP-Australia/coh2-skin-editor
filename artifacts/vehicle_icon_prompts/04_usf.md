Create a single flat 2D line-art icon sheet of 17 World War II US Forces military vehicles, all in ONE identical minimalist UI-icon style.

STYLE (identical on every icon):
- Pure outline line art like Lucide / Feather UI icons. Uniform thin stroke (about 1.5px at 24px scale), exactly the same weight on every icon. Rounded stroke caps and rounded joins.
- NO fill, NO shading, NO gradients, NO color. Pure white (#FFFFFF) strokes only.
- Background: solid PURE BLACK (#000000), perfectly flat and uniform — no vignette, no gradient, no noise. (Keyed out by luminance later; keep black even, strokes pure white.)
- Clean LEFT-FACING side profile, simplified to an icon but HISTORICALLY ACCURATE in proportion and distinguishing features. Readable small.
- Every icon the SAME visual size: scale each vehicle to fill the same cell, centered, equal padding and gutters.

ACCURACY RULES:
- HULL FRONT: as the real vehicle.
- RUNNING GEAR: American tanks (Sherman/Stuart/Pershing families) ride on MANY SMALL PAIRED bogie wheels in distinct bogie units (Easy 8 has noticeably WIDER tracks and spaced HVSS bogies); halftracks have front wheels + a short rear track; armored cars roll on 6 rubber tyres.
- TURRET + GUN: turret silhouette (rounded/open-top/none) and gun type & length are the primary identifiers; open-top tank destroyers vs enclosed tanks is a key cue.
- SIGNATURE FEATURE: include the one or two standout features that distinguish each from its near-identical neighbours.

LAYOUT: one image, a uniform grid of 6 columns × 3 rows, equal cells and gutters, filled left-to-right top-to-bottom in order. Leave trailing cell empty.

ABSOLUTE RULES: NO text, letters, numbers, labels, captions. NO insignia, flags, stars. NO drop shadow, NO 3D, NO perspective, NO background beyond flat black. Identical thin white stroke weight on every icon. NO external stowage boxes, bins, baskets, racks, jerry cans, crates, tools or spare track links on the turret or hull — keep every vehicle a clean plain silhouette.

THE 17 VEHICLES (left-facing line-art side profiles):

1. Pershing (M26) — Large, heavy medium tank on torsion-bar individual road wheels: six large single road wheels per side, evenly spaced with no bogie grouping, wide tracks. Long hull, prominent rounded cast turret set well back, very long 90mm gun with muzzle brake projecting far forward. Noticeably bigger and heavier-looking than any Sherman variant. Signature: six individual large road wheels (not bogies) + very long 90mm gun.

2. Easy 8 (M4A3E8) — Sherman hull with the unmistakable HVSS wide-track suspension: four widely spaced bogie units, each bogie being a broader paired-wheel unit with a horizontal volute, giving a visibly wider, chunkier running gear compared to VVSS Shermans. Welded hull with 47-degree sloped glacis. Rounded cast turret (same large 76mm turret as Sherman 76mm). Muzzle brake on the 76mm gun. Signature: distinctly WIDE HVSS bogies — the widest-looking track run of all Sherman variants.

3. Sherman 76mm (M4A3 76mm) — Standard Sherman silhouette on narrow VVSS suspension: three bogie units per side, each a paired small-wheel volute bogie, narrow tracks. 47-degree welded glacis hull. Large rounded turret (T23 turret, taller and more rounded than the 75mm turret) with a noticeably long 76mm gun and muzzle brake. Signature: long 76mm gun + large rounded T23 turret on narrow VVSS track run (narrower than Easy 8).

4. Calliope (M4A1 Sherman + T34 rocket launcher) — Sherman hull on narrow VVSS bogies, but dominated by a massive multi-tube rocket rack mounted above and forward of the turret: two rectangular banks of launch tubes (36 tubes in two stacked rows) extending well forward, connected to the 75mm gun barrel by a linkage arm. Turret is a standard rounded 75mm Sherman turret mostly hidden by the rack. Signature: huge protruding rectangular tube rack above the turret — unmistakable.

5. M10 Wolverine — Sherman-derived hull on VVSS narrow bogies, but with a distinctive open-top pentagonal welded turret: five flat angled sides forming a low angular pentagon when seen in profile, no roof, gun shield at the front. Medium-length 3-inch (76mm) gun. Rear of turret has a prominent counterweight overhang. Hull sits lower at the rear. Signature: angular flat-sided open-top pentagonal turret with visible counterweight at the rear bustle.

6. M36 Jackson — Similar Sherman-chassis hull to the M10 but carrying a larger rounded open-top turret with a prominent rear bustle/counterweight. The turret sides are curved rather than flat and angular. The gun is significantly longer than the M10's — the long 90mm M3 gun projecting well forward. Signature: LONGER 90mm gun (longer than M10's) + rounder open-top turret with a large cast counterweight bustle at the rear.

7. M5 Stuart — Small, compact light tank. Low rounded hull with sloped glacis. Raised rear engine deck. VVSS suspension with four bogie units per side (more bogies, smaller wheel pairs, finer spacing than Sherman). Small rounded fully enclosed turret with a short 37mm gun. Low overall silhouette. Signature: four VVSS bogie pairs (more numerous, closely spaced), compact low hull, short 37mm gun.

8. Greyhound (M8 armored car) — Six-wheeled wheeled armored car: three rubber-tyred axles visible in profile (front, mid, rear), no tracks. Low sloped hull. Small rounded enclosed turret (open-top at the rear but turret has a partial roof over the gunner) with a short 37mm gun. Signature: six rubber wheels (no tracks) + small compact rounded turret with short gun — clearly wheeled not tracked.

9. Priest (M7B1) — Open-top self-propelled howitzer on a Sherman M4A3 chassis with VVSS narrow bogies. No turret — instead the gun is mounted in a fixed open superstructure. The howitzer points at a slight upward angle. MOST DISTINCTIVE: a tall cylindrical pulpit-style armored MG ring mount rises at the right front corner of the superstructure, clearly protruding above the hull. Signature: cylindrical raised MG pulpit ring at the right front corner — unlike any other vehicle.

10. M3 Halftrack — Classic halftrack profile: two large rubber-tyred front wheels under a short bonnet/cab, then an open-top armored troop compartment over a short rear track (rubber-padded track with small road wheels). Open-top with low side armour. Single pintle MG at the front of the troop bay. Signature: front wheels + rear short track + open rectangular troop compartment — the baseline halftrack shape all others are compared to.

11. AA Halftrack (M16 MGMC) — Same M3 halftrack hull and running gear as #10, but the rear open troop bay is dominated by the M45 Quadmount: a large rotating pedestal mount holding four .50 cal barrels fanned out side by side, the barrels and mount clearly visible above the hull sides. The Maxson mount's four parallel barrels are the unmistakable cue. Signature: four parallel .50 cal barrels in the Maxson quad mount rising prominently from the rear bed.

12. M8 Scott — M5 Stuart chassis (same four VVSS bogie pairs, same compact hull) but with a new large open-top turret holding a short, wide-bore 75mm howitzer. The turret is wider and more prominent than the Stuart's small 37mm turret, and is open-topped. A .50 cal MG sits at the rear right of the turret ring. Signature: Stuart-sized hull + large open-top turret with a short fat 75mm howitzer barrel (clearly shorter and fatter than any of the Sherman guns).

13. M20 — Same six-wheeled hull as the Greyhound M8 (#8): three rubber-tyred axles, no tracks, same low sloped hull. BUT there is NO turret — instead the top is an open low-sided armored compartment with a ring-mounted .50 cal MG visible as a simple pedestal and gun above the hull. Significantly lower profile than the Greyhound. Signature: six rubber wheels (same as Greyhound) but flat open-top with no turret — just the ring MG pedestal above the hull line.

14. M21 Mortar HT — M3 halftrack hull and running gear (front wheels + rear short track, open troop bay), but with an 81mm mortar mounted in the rear bed, barrel pointing forward and upward at a steep angle. The mortar tube's steep upward angle dominates the rear compartment silhouette. Signature: steep-angle mortar tube pointing forward-upward from the rear bed — distinguishes it from the flat M3 and the quad-gun AA halftrack.

15. Dodge WC51 — Small open 3/4-ton 4x4 truck: four rubber tyres (two axles), short bonnet with rounded engine hood, open cab with no roof (folded top), flat cargo bed at the rear with low sides. Smaller and more truck-like than any halftrack; no tracks. Signature: completely open cab, short 4-wheel truck with flat cargo bed — clearly a wheeled utility truck, not armored.

16. WC54 Ambulance — Same WC-series Dodge chassis and running gear as #15 (four rubber tyres, two axles, same short bonneted front), but the open cargo bed is replaced by a tall, boxy enclosed sheet-metal ambulance body — a high rectangular box body with a straight roofline. Noticeably taller than the WC51. Signature: tall rectangular enclosed box body over the rear — the only fully enclosed box-body vehicle in the roster.

17. M4A3 Sherman — The baseline Sherman: VVSS narrow suspension with three bogie units per side (small paired wheels), narrow track run. 47-degree sloped welded glacis. Standard rounded 75mm turret — smaller and lower than the 76mm T23 turret, with a shorter 75mm gun and no muzzle brake. Signature: shorter 75mm gun with no muzzle brake + smaller rounder turret than #3 (Sherman 76mm), narrow VVSS bogies — the most "classic" Sherman silhouette.

Final reminder: identical thin white line-art stroke on every icon, flat pure-black background, NO text anywhere.
