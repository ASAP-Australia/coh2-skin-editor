Create a single flat 2D line-art icon sheet of 13 World War II German Oberkommando West (OKW) military vehicles, all in ONE identical minimalist UI-icon style.

STYLE (identical on every icon):
- Pure outline line art like Lucide / Feather UI icons. Uniform thin stroke (about 1.5px at 24px scale), exactly the same weight on every icon. Rounded stroke caps and rounded joins.
- NO fill, NO shading, NO gradients, NO color. Pure white (#FFFFFF) strokes only.
- Background: solid PURE BLACK (#000000), perfectly flat and uniform across the entire image — no vignette, no gradient, no noise. (Keyed out by luminance later, so keep black even and strokes pure white.)
- Clean LEFT-FACING side profile of each vehicle, simplified to an icon but HISTORICALLY ACCURATE in proportion and distinguishing features. Readable at small size.
- Every icon the SAME visual size: scale each vehicle to fill the same cell, centered, equal padding and equal gutters.

ACCURACY RULES:
- HULL FRONT: flat vertical front vs steeply sloped glacis — exactly as the real vehicle.
- RUNNING GEAR: German tanks ride on LARGE OVERLAPPING / INTERLEAVED road wheels with no visible gaps; halftracks have front steering wheels + a long rear track unit; the Puma armored car has EIGHT wheels.
- TURRET + GUN: turret silhouette (boxy/rounded/sloped/open-top/none) and gun type & length (long thin AT gun vs short fat howitzer vs stubby fat mortar vs autocannon) are the primary identifiers.
- SIGNATURE FEATURE: include the one or two standout features that distinguish each from its neighbours.

LAYOUT: one image, a uniform grid of 5 columns × 3 rows, equal cells and gutters, filled left-to-right top-to-bottom in the listed order. Leave trailing cells empty.

ABSOLUTE RULES: NO text, letters, numbers, labels, or captions. NO insignia, flags, crosses, or stars. NO drop shadow, NO 3D, NO perspective, NO background beyond the flat black fill. Identical thin white stroke weight on every icon. NO external stowage boxes, bins, baskets, racks, jerry cans, crates, tools or spare track links on the turret or hull — keep every vehicle a clean plain silhouette.

THE 13 VEHICLES (left-facing line-art side profiles):

1. King Tiger — Very large, heavily armoured tank. Hull front is a steeply sloped glacis (50°), NOT flat — this distinguishes it immediately from the Tiger I. Large overlapping/interleaved road wheels, six visible per side. Turret is angular with a sloped front face. Gun is very long and thin: the 88mm KwK 43 L/71, projecting well past the front of the hull — noticeably longer than a Panther's gun. Signature: extreme combined length (long hull + long gun overhang) and steeply raked glacis.

2. Jagdtiger — The largest and heaviest WW2 German AFV. Built on a lengthened King Tiger hull with large overlapping road wheels. NO turret — instead a massive, tall, boxy rectangular casemate superstructure that spans almost the full hull length, with vertical sides and a nearly flat roof. Gun is enormous: the 128mm PaK 44, protruding as a very long, thick barrel well ahead of the hull front. Signature: colossal casemate height + extreme-length huge-diameter gun barrel — unmistakably the biggest vehicle in the set.

3. Sturmtiger — Built on a Tiger I hull with large overlapping road wheels. Superstructure is a tall, boxy, near-cubic casemate with steeply sloped front plate and nearly vertical sides. The weapon is a 380mm rocket mortar (RW 61): depicted as an extremely short, very wide/fat barrel stub barely protruding from the front of the casemate — roughly as wide as it is long, with a distinctive ring of gas-vent holes around the muzzle. Signature: massive fat stubby barrel — completely unlike every other vehicle's gun; looks like a drainpipe end, not a gun barrel.

4. Panther — Medium-heavy tank. Hull has a distinctively long, steeply sloped glacis (55°) giving a low front profile. Large overlapping/interleaved road wheels. Turret is rounded/oval with a sloped front mantlet, sitting mid-hull. Gun is long and thin: 75mm KwK 42 L/70, projecting well ahead of the hull. Signature: sleek low sloped hull + very long thin gun; lower and more streamlined than King Tiger, smaller than King Tiger overall.

5. Jagdpanzer IV — Very low-slung turretless casemate tank destroyer on Panzer IV chassis. The entire superstructure is a flat, wide, steeply angled wedge — front plate sloped at ~45°, sides slope outward, roof very low. Interleaved road wheels (smaller than Panther). Carries a long 75mm gun (L/48 or L/70) in a small mantlet flush with the sloped front plate. Signature: extremely low profile — almost flat to the ground — with a sleek sloped casemate, no turret, and a long gun close to hull centerline; looks like an angular ramp.

6. Panzer IV — Medium tank with a clearly boxy, upright rectangular hull with a nearly vertical front glacis. Road wheels are small, evenly spaced, and non-overlapping (four large road wheels per side — distinct from interleaved German heavies). Small cylindrical turret with a flat face, mid-hull. Gun is medium-length: 75mm KwK 40 L/48. Signature: flat rectangular side-skirt armor plates (Schürzen) hanging from rails along both hull sides covering the upper track run — a continuous flat panel above the tracks is the single most distinctive feature.

7. Hetzer — Tiny, very low, compact tank destroyer. The entire vehicle is a sharply angled wedge shape: front plate slopes steeply backward at ~60°, hull sides converge toward the rear, giving a triangular side profile. Very small — barely taller than a man. Carries a 75mm gun in an offset mantlet on the right side of the sloped front plate. Road wheels are small and closely spaced (based on Pz38(t) chassis). Signature: extreme wedge/ramp shape with steeply converging sides, tiny size, and offset gun mount — unmistakably small and angular.

8. Puma — Eight-wheeled (8-rad) heavy armored car. Long, low hull riding on EIGHT evenly-spaced large road wheels (four per side visible in profile), all-wheel drive. No tracks at all. Hull is low and streamlined. Small fully-enclosed turret (designed for the Leopard light tank) with a rounded front and a medium-length 50mm KwK 39/1 L/60 gun with a prominent "pig's-head" rounded mantlet. Signature: eight large wheels with NO tracks — the wheel arrangement is unique in the entire set.

9. Luchs — Small light reconnaissance tank. Hull is compact and low. Running gear uses overlapping/interleaved road wheels (five per side), smaller than Panther/Tiger wheels. Small, rounded low-profile turret with a short 20mm autocannon (KwK 38 L/55) — distinctly short gun compared to the tank destroyers. Signature: small overall size + interleaved wheels + very short stubby autocannon barrel; the smallest tracked vehicle in the set.

10. Kübelwagen — Very small, lightweight open-topped utility car (Volkswagen Typ 82). Four small, widely-spaced road wheels, NO tracks. Simple boxy rectangular body with a flat hood at front, open passenger compartment/bench seating visible in profile, spare wheel often visible on rear. No armor plating, no gun. Signature: tiny four-wheeled open jeep-like silhouette — smallest vehicle in the set by far, clearly unarmed and unarmored.

11. Sd.Kfz. 251 — Standard German medium halftrack (Ausf. D). Front has two steerable road wheels under an angled armored hull nose. Long rear track unit with small road wheels. Open-topped armored troop compartment with sloped sides and a flat rear door. Low angled front armor plate. No prominent mounted weapon (base APC version). Signature: halftrack layout — wheels at front, long tracks at rear — with the characteristic sloped-nose armored body.

12. Sd.Kfz. 251 Flak — Identical halftrack hull and running gear as #11. Open-topped rear compartment. An anti-aircraft gun (20mm Flak 38 or similar) mounted on a pintle/ring mount in the open rear, with a visible thin-barrelled AA gun angled upward and a curved gun shield. Signature: same halftrack body as #11 but with a prominent elevated AA gun barrel angling skyward from the open rear — distinguishes it from the plain 251.

13. Sd.Kfz. 251 IR — Identical halftrack hull and running gear as #11 and #12. Open-topped rear compartment. Carries a LARGE, PROMINENT circular infrared searchlight dish (the "Uhu" — Sd.Kfz. 251/20) mounted on a pedestal/mount in the open rear compartment, facing forward-left. The dish is wide and round — like a large circular reflector or radar dish, clearly not a gun. Signature: the unmistakable large round dish/reflector dominating the rear of the vehicle — completely unlike the gun or AA mount on the other two 251 variants.

Final reminder: identical thin white line-art stroke on every icon, flat pure-black background, NO text anywhere.
