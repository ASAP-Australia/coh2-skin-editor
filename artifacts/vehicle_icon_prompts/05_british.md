Create a single flat 2D line-art icon sheet of 9 World War II British (UKF) military vehicles, all in ONE identical minimalist UI-icon style.

STYLE (identical on every icon):
- Pure outline line art like Lucide / Feather UI icons. Uniform thin stroke (about 1.5px at 24px scale), exactly the same weight on every icon. Rounded stroke caps and rounded joins.
- NO fill, NO shading, NO gradients, NO color. Pure white (#FFFFFF) strokes only.
- Background: solid PURE BLACK (#000000), perfectly flat and uniform — no vignette, no gradient, no noise. (Keyed out by luminance later; keep black even, strokes pure white.)
- Clean LEFT-FACING side profile, simplified to an icon but HISTORICALLY ACCURATE in proportion and distinguishing features. Readable small.
- Every icon the SAME visual size: scale each vehicle to fill the same cell, centered, equal padding and gutters.

ACCURACY RULES:
- HULL FRONT: as the real vehicle.
- RUNNING GEAR: British cruiser tanks (Cromwell, Comet) have several medium evenly-spaced large road wheels (Christie type); the Churchill has MANY small road wheels with its track running the FULL LENGTH and up OVER THE TOP of the hull; the Firefly is a Sherman on many small bogie wheels; wheeled cars have rubber tyres.
- TURRET + GUN: turret silhouette (boxy/rounded/open-top/none) and gun type & length are the primary identifiers.
- SIGNATURE FEATURE: include the one or two standout features distinguishing each from its neighbours.

LAYOUT: one image, a uniform grid of 3 columns × 3 rows, equal cells and gutters, filled left-to-right top-to-bottom in order.

ABSOLUTE RULES: NO text, letters, numbers, labels, captions. NO insignia, flags, stars. NO drop shadow, NO 3D, NO perspective, NO background beyond flat black. Identical thin white stroke weight on every icon. NO external stowage boxes, bins, baskets, racks, jerry cans, crates, tools or spare track links on the turret or hull — keep every vehicle a clean plain silhouette.

THE 9 VEHICLES (left-facing line-art side profiles):

1. Churchill — Massive, very long, very low infantry tank. The track runs the FULL LENGTH of the hull bottom AND curves up and over the top of the hull forming a continuous loop above the hull roof — the single most distinctive feature of any tank here. Many (about 11) tiny road wheels visible as a tight row along the bottom of the track. Hull is a long flat-topped slab, nearly rectangular. Small, boxy turret sits centrally on top — its footprint is narrow relative to the long hull, mounting a short-to-medium 75mm gun. Signature: over-the-top full-length track loop + row of tiny wheels + small turret on a very long low hull.

2. Comet — Sleek British cruiser tank, more refined than the Cromwell it evolved from. Hull has a noticeably sloped lower front glacis. Five large, evenly spaced road wheels (Christie type) with a clear gap between hull floor and track. Turret is well-rounded with gentle curves, mounting a long 77mm HV gun that protrudes well beyond the front of the hull — gun length is longer than a Cromwell's 75mm. Hull sides have a slight taper toward the rear. Signature: sloped front hull + rounded turret + distinctly long gun overhang.

3. Cromwell — Boxy, fairly tall British cruiser tank. Vertical or near-vertical hull sides with a flat roof. Five large evenly-spaced road wheels (Christie suspension — large diameter, clearly separated). Turret is boxy/rectangular with a flat roof and a medium-length 75mm gun that does NOT protrude as far as the Comet's. Hull front is more vertical than the Comet's. Overall silhouette is squarer and taller relative to length than the Comet. Signature: tall boxy rectangular hull + boxy turret + shorter gun than Comet + large Christie wheels.

4. Centaur AA — Cromwell/Centaur hull (same boxy hull and large Christie road wheels as icon 3) but with a completely OPEN-TOP turret: the turret ring is visible from the side, the turret walls are low, and TWIN anti-aircraft autocannons (Polsten 20mm) mount side by side, angled upward at roughly 30–45 degrees — they protrude up and forward, not level. No closed turret roof at all. Signature: open-top turret with twin upward-angled AA gun barrels above a Cromwell-type hull.

5. Firefly — A Sherman M4 tank hull — recognisable by its tall, rounded cast hull front and the row of many small paired bogie wheels (three bogies of two wheels each, six wheels total) along the bottom. The turret is a standard Sherman-style rounded turret, BUT the gun is an extremely long 17-pounder (QF 17 pdr) that extends FAR forward of the hull — noticeably longer than any other gun in this sheet. The gun length is the single most obvious identifier. Signature: Sherman bogie suspension + very long gun (roughly 1.5× hull length total with overhang).

6. Valentine — Small, compact, low infantry tank. Clearly smaller overall than the Cromwell or Churchill. Three large road wheels visible per side (Valentine uses a distinctive layout of three pairs of large wheels, giving a wide-spaced look). Hull is relatively low-profile with a flat top. Turret is small and rounded, mounting a short 2-pounder or 6-pounder gun (short-to-medium length). The whole vehicle is notably narrow and compact — smaller than any other tracked tank here except the Bren Carrier. Signature: three large widely-spaced road wheels + small rounded turret + compact low overall silhouette.

7. Sexton SPG — Open-top self-propelled gun on a Ram/Grizzly (Canadian Sherman-derived) hull. The hull is medium-length. Running gear shows several medium bogie-style wheels. The fighting compartment at the REAR of the hull is open-topped — no roof — and contains a 25-pounder (QF 25 pdr) field gun on a pedestal mount that points forward and slightly upward. The gun barrel is medium-length and thick, pointing forward-up at about 10–20 degrees above horizontal. No turret — just an open armoured box at the rear with the gun pointing out the front. Signature: open-top rear fighting compartment + forward-pointing gun elevated slightly + no turret.

8. AEC Armoured Car — Heavy four-wheeled (4x4) armoured car. Two large rubber-tyred road wheels visible on the left side (front and rear axles). The hull is boxy and relatively tall for a wheeled vehicle. Crucially, it mounts a PROPER TANK-LIKE TURRET — large, rounded, similar in size to a light tank turret — with a 6-pounder (or 75mm) gun of medium length. The turret is disproportionately large relative to the wheeled hull, making it instantly distinguishable from lighter armoured cars. Signature: two large rubber wheels + large tank-style turret with a real gun + boxy hull.

9. Universal Carrier (Bren Carrier) — The smallest vehicle on the sheet by a wide margin. Very low, very short, open-top tracked carrier with no turret whatsoever. Slab-sided boxy hull, nearly rectangular in profile. A small number of road wheels (typically 3–4 small wheels visible). The top of the hull is completely open — the crew compartment has no roof. Overall height is roughly half that of the Valentine. Gun (if shown) is a light Bren gun or Boys AT rifle barely visible above the hull lip. Signature: tiny, very low, open-top box on tracks with no turret — smallest tracked vehicle here.

Final reminder: identical thin white line-art stroke on every icon, flat pure-black background, NO text anywhere.
